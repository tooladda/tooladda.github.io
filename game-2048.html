2048 rules — how to play

  1. The game starts with two tiles (2 or 4) on a 4×4 grid.
  2. Use arrow keys, WASD, swipe, or the D-pad to slide all tiles in one direction.
  3. When two tiles with the same number collide, they merge into one tile with double the value.
  4. After every valid move, a new 2 (90% chance) or 4 (10% chance) spawns in a random empty cell.
  5. You win when you create a 2048 tile — but you can keep playing for higher scores.
  6. The game ends when the board is full and no adjacent tiles can merge.

How scoring works in 2048

Your score increases every time two tiles merge. You earn points equal to the value of the newly created tile. For example, merging two 512 tiles into 1024 adds 1024 points to your score. Bigger merges mean bigger score jumps — reaching 2048 typically requires a score of 20,000+ depending on your path.

Best 2048 strategy tips

  • Corner method — lock your highest tile in the bottom-right (or any corner) and never move it away.
  • Monotonic rows — keep values decreasing along the bottom row toward your corner.
  • Two-direction discipline — primarily use down and right; only go up/left when forced.
  • Maintain empty space — always try to keep at least one empty row or column.
  • Don't chase random merges — plan 2–3 moves ahead before sliding.
  • Use Undo wisely — recover when a slide scatters your large tiles.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Moving in all four directions randomly instead of sticking to a pattern.
  • Letting a large tile drift away from your chosen corner.
  • Filling the board without keeping merge paths open.
  • Rushing moves — one bad slide can end a 30-minute run.
  • Ignoring the 4-spawns — they can block your corner if you're not careful.

History of the 2048 game

2048 was created by Italian web developer Gabriele Cirulli in March 2014 and released as an open-source browser game. It went viral within days, accumulating millions of plays. The game was inspired by the earlier mobile game Threes! but simplified to powers of two on a 4×4 grid. Today, 2048 remains one of the most played browser puzzles worldwide, with countless clones and variations.

2048 vs Threes vs 1024

  • 2048 — powers of 2 (2, 4, 8…2048), 4×4 grid, merge any matching pair.
  • Threes! — adds 1 and 2 tiles that combine to 3; more complex scoring.
  • 1024 — similar to 2048 but goal is 1024; fewer merges needed.

Frequently asked questions about 2048

What is the 2048 game?

2048 is a sliding tile puzzle on a 4×4 grid. Merge matching numbers to double their value — 2 becomes 4, 4 becomes 8, and so on — until you reach 2048 or run out of moves.

How does scoring work in 2048?

What is the best strategy to win 2048?

Keep your highest tile locked in one corner, build descending values along one edge, use primarily two directions, and maintain empty cells for flexibility.

Who invented 2048?

Gabriele Cirulli, an Italian web developer, created 2048 in March 2014. It became a viral sensation within days of release.

Is 2048 the same as Threes?

No. Threes uses 1, 2, and 3 tiles with different merge rules. 2048 uses powers of two (2, 4, 8…) on a 4×4 grid and was inspired by Threes but plays differently.
