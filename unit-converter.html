    What This Unit Converter Does

    A unit converter turns a measurement in one unit into the same measurement in another. This one covers fifteen categories and more than a hundred units, and it is built around three things most converters skip:

    • Exact factors, not rounded ones. An inch is exactly 0.0254 m and a pound is exactly 0.45359237 kg by international agreement. Those definitions are used verbatim, so errors do not accumulate.
    • Scales that are not simple ratios. Temperature has an offset and fuel economy is inverted. Multiplying by a single factor gives the wrong answer for both, so each is handled with its real formula.
    • Units that share a name. A ton, a gallon, a calorie and a horsepower all mean different things in different places. Each variant is listed separately with a note, so you are never guessing which one you got.

    The all-units table shows your value in every unit of a category at once, which usually answers the follow-up question before you ask it.

    Four Conversions People Get Wrong

    Temperature is not a ratio. Celsius and Fahrenheit have different zero points, so you cannot scale between them. The formula is °F = °C × 9/5 + 32. A quick sanity check: −40 °C and −40 °F are the same temperature, and any converter that disagrees is broken.

    Fuel economy runs backwards. Miles per gallon measures distance per fuel; litres per 100 km measures fuel per distance. A higher mpg means a lower L/100km, so the conversion is a division: L/100km = 235.21 ÷ mpg. And US and UK mpg differ by about 20% because they use different gallons.

    Your hard drive is not lying to you. Manufacturers count a terabyte as a trillion bytes; most operating systems count in units of 1,024. One trillion bytes is 931.32 GiB, so a "1 TB" drive genuinely holds what it says — the two systems just count differently. Decimal and binary units are listed separately here.

    Megabits are not megabytes. Internet plans are sold in megabits per second, download managers report megabytes per second, and there are eight bits in a byte. A 100 Mbps line tops out around 12.5 MB/s.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is a unit converter?

    A unit converter changes a measurement from one unit into another — kilometres into miles, kilograms into pounds, Celsius into Fahrenheit. This one covers fifteen categories and more than a hundred units, and shows the value in every unit of a category at once.

    How do I convert km to miles?

    Multiply kilometres by 0.621371. One mile is exactly 1,609.344 metres by international definition, so 100 km is 62.14 miles. Pick Length, set the units to kilometre and mile, and the tool does it as you type.

    How do I convert kg to lbs?

    Multiply kilograms by 2.20462. One pound is exactly 0.45359237 kilograms, which is an exact definition rather than a rounded figure, so 70 kg is 154.32 lb.

    How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

    Multiply by 9/5 and add 32, so 20 °C is 68 °F. Temperature is not a simple ratio because the scales have different zero points, which is why a converter that only multiplies gets it wrong. A useful check: −40 °C and −40 °F are the same temperature.

    Why is a US gallon different from a UK gallon?

    They are simply different units that share a name. A US gallon is 3.785411784 litres and an imperial gallon is 4.54609 litres, so the UK gallon is about 20 percent larger. Both are listed separately here so you never have to guess which one is meant.

    What is the difference between a US ton and a UK ton?

    A US short ton is 2,000 pounds and a UK long ton is 2,240 pounds, while a metric ton is 1,000 kilograms. All three are listed separately, because picking the wrong one is a 10 percent error.

    Why does my 1 TB hard drive show as 931 GB?

    Because two different definitions are in use. Drive makers sell a terabyte as one trillion bytes, while operating systems usually count in binary units of 1,024. One trillion bytes is 931.32 gibibytes, so nothing is missing — the two systems are just counting differently. This tool lists decimal and binary units separately so you can see both.

    How do I convert Mbps to MB/s?

    Divide by eight, because there are eight bits in a byte. A 100 Mbps connection downloads at about 12.5 MB/s at best. Internet plans are advertised in megabits while download managers usually report megabytes, which is why the numbers look so different.

    How do I convert mpg to L/100km?

    Divide 235.21 by the US mpg figure, so 30 mpg is about 7.84 L/100km. Fuel economy is inverse: a higher mpg means a lower L/100km, so you cannot convert it by multiplying. The tool handles that inversion for you.

    Is a US mpg the same as a UK mpg?

    No. They use different gallons, so the same car scores about 20 percent higher in UK mpg than in US mpg. Both are listed separately in the fuel economy category.

    How accurate are these conversions?

    Every factor is either an exact international definition — the inch, foot, mile, pound, gallon, atmosphere and nautical mile all have one — or is marked with a note explaining the assumption. Results are computed at full double precision and only rounded for display, and you control how much.

    Can I control the number of decimal places?

    What units does the converter support?

    Length, weight and mass, temperature, volume, area, speed, time, digital storage, data transfer, pressure, energy, power, angle, frequency and fuel economy — fifteen categories in total, with more than a hundred individual units.
