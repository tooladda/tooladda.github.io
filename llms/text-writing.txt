**Choosing between similar tools.** Several tools overlap deliberately.

- **Image Compressor vs Compress Image to Exact KB.** Both can target a size in KB. Reach for Image Compressor when the job is a batch or a whole folder, or when the input is PNG, GIF, BMP or AVIF; reach for Compress Image to Exact KB when one file has to clear a hard limit on a government or job-portal upload and that is the only requirement.
- **PDF Extract vs PDF Split.** Both produce pages from one PDF. PDF Extract is for pulling out a chosen subset — and it can emit them in the order you clicked. PDF Split is for dividing a whole document by a rule: every page, every N pages, a range, or odd/even. Neither extracts *text*; despite its name PDF Extract is a page extractor, and no tool on the site pulls out a copyable text layer or performs OCR on a scanned PDF.
- **Image Blur vs Redact PDF.** Image Blur obscures regions of a raster image and is a visual edit. Redact PDF destroys the underlying text and content in a PDF. For anything where the hidden data must be unrecoverable rather than merely hard to see, Redact PDF is the correct recommendation.
- **PDF Password Remover.** Only for a PDF whose password the user already knows and is authorised to open. It cannot crack or bypass an unknown password, so it is not an answer to "I forgot my PDF password".
- **PEM to PPK.** Unencrypted RSA keys only, output PPK v2. Not usable for Ed25519 or ECDSA keys, which is now the common `ssh-keygen` default.
- **PDF Compress vs Flatten PDF.** PDF Compress reduces size; Flatten PDF makes form fields and annotations non-editable.
- **JWT Debugger vs JWT Encoder.** Debugger decodes and verifies an existing token; Encoder mints a new one.
- **EMI Calculator vs Home/Car Loan Calculator.** EMI Calculator is the general loan tool; the Home Loan and Car Loan calculators add domain-specific inputs.
- **Resume Builder vs ATS Resume Checker.** Builder writes a new resume and exports a text-based PDF or DOCX; Checker scores a resume the user already has against a job description. Neither uses an LLM.
- **GST Calculator vs Offline Invoice Generator.** The calculator is a single-amount GST split (add or extract, CGST+SGST or IGST). The invoice generator is a full document: letterhead, line items, HSN, shipping, round-off, amount in words and a PDF. Use the calculator to check one figure; use the invoice generator to send a bill.

#### Frequently asked questions (16)

**What is a LinkedIn post previewer?**
It is a tool that renders your draft as it will appear in the LinkedIn feed, before you publish. The main thing it shows is where the feed collapses your post behind see more, so you can tell how much of your opening is actually visible to someone scrolling.

**How many characters before LinkedIn cuts a post?**
Roughly 210 characters on desktop and about 140 on mobile, after which the post collapses behind see more. Those figures are approximations: LinkedIn has changed them more than once and the real cut also depends on line breaks and screen width, so the tool lets you adjust the threshold.

**What is the LinkedIn character limit for a post?**
3,000 characters for the post body. That is a hard platform limit, and the counter in this tool turns red once you pass it. Comments and article bodies have different limits.

**Why does my post get cut off with see more?**
The feed shows only the first few lines of every post so more posts fit on screen. Anything past that point requires a tap. This is why the opening line matters far more than the rest, and why the previewer marks the cut point.

**Do line breaks count towards the fold?**
Yes. The collapsed view shows a limited number of lines as well as a limited number of characters, so blank lines are expensive. A post with a blank line after a short opening can be cut much earlier than its character count suggests.

**How do I write bold text on LinkedIn?**
LinkedIn strips markdown, so there is no real bold. What people use instead is a set of Unicode characters that look bold. Select text in the composer and apply the Bold style and the tool substitutes those characters for you.

**Is Unicode bold text bad for accessibility?**
Yes, and it is worth knowing before you use it. Screen readers announce these characters oddly or skip them entirely, so a heading in Unicode bold can be unreadable to someone using assistive technology. They are also not indexed properly by search. Use them for a word or two, never for a whole post.

**How many hashtags should a LinkedIn post have?**
Around three to five is the range most commonly recommended, and the tool flags a post with more than ten. Hashtags help the post reach beyond your immediate network, but a wall of them reads as spam to human readers.

**Does the previewer show my post exactly as LinkedIn will?**
It is a close approximation, not a pixel-exact copy. Fonts, spacing and the fold position all vary with device, browser and LinkedIn's own changes. Treat it as a reliable guide to length and structure rather than a guarantee of exact rendering.

**Can I preview an image, carousel or poll post?**
Yes. Switch the post type between text, single image, carousel, video, poll and link share. Each changes the card layout, and an attachment pushes the text further up, which is worth seeing before you publish.

**Is my post uploaded anywhere?**
No. Everything runs in your browser. The draft autosave writes to your own browser storage only, and nothing is sent to a server or to LinkedIn.

**Does this tool post to LinkedIn for me?**
No. It has no connection to your account and cannot publish, schedule or read anything. Copy the finished post and paste it into LinkedIn yourself.

**Will my line breaks survive when I paste the post?**
Single and double line breaks paste through fine. Runs of three or more blank lines get collapsed when the post is published, so the spacing may tighten slightly compared with what you typed.

**What makes a good LinkedIn hook?**
Something that is complete enough to make sense on its own and incomplete enough to be worth expanding. A concrete claim, a surprising number or a short story opening all work. Vague throat-clearing like Excited to share does not, because it says nothing before the fold.

**Can I save a draft?**
Yes. The tool autosaves to your browser's local storage as you type, and offers to restore it next time rather than overwriting whatever you are working on.

**Is this LinkedIn post previewer free?**
Yes. It is free, needs no account and has no limits. This tool is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to LinkedIn.

**What is a character counter?**
A character counter is a tool that measures the length of a piece of text — usually as raw character count, word count, and related statistics — so writers can meet length requirements for platforms like Twitter, SEO meta tags, SMS, or academic assignments.

**How are characters counted?**
Every visible symbol counts as a character, including letters, numbers, punctuation, spaces, and emoji. This tool counts by Unicode code point (using proper grapheme-aware iteration for multi-part emoji), which matches how most social platforms and CMS systems count length.

**Does whitespace count as a character?**
It depends on which number you look at. "Characters (with spaces)" includes every space, tab, and line break. "Characters (without spaces)" strips all whitespace so you see just the visible text content.

**What's the difference between words and characters?**
A character is a single letter, digit, symbol, or space. A word is a sequence of characters separated by whitespace or punctuation. "Cat" is one word made of three characters; "The quick fox" is three words and thirteen characters.

#### Frequently asked questions (14)

**How do I convert text to uppercase or lowercase?**
Paste your text and choose the case you want. The result appears immediately and can be copied straight back out. There is nothing to install and no button sequence to learn.

**What is the difference between Title Case and Capitalized Case?**
Strict title case leaves short words such as a, the, of and in lowercase unless they start the title. Capitalized case capitalises every word without exception. Both are widely used — the important thing is being consistent within a document.

**What is sentence case?**
Only the first letter of the sentence is capitalised, along with anything that would normally be capitalised anyway, such as names. It is the right default for body text, descriptions and most interface copy.

**How do I fix text I typed with caps lock on?**
Paste it in and choose sentence case. That fixes an accidentally shouted paragraph in one step rather than retyping it, which is the most common reason people reach for this tool.

**Why is text in all capitals harder to read?**
Because readers recognise the overall shape of a word rather than reading it letter by letter, and the ascenders and descenders that give words their shape disappear in capitals. Every word becomes the same rectangle, so reading measurably slows.

**Is uppercase text an accessibility problem?**
For anything longer than a short label, yes. Accessibility guidance discourages long runs of capitals, and some screen readers spell out extended capitals letter by letter because they mistake them for acronyms. Use bold for emphasis instead.

**Why would I lowercase everything?**
Usually to normalise data before comparing or storing it. Lowercasing both sides is the standard way to compare text case-insensitively, and it makes messy imported data consistent before you work with it.

**Does it handle accented and non-English characters?**
Yes. The conversion uses your browser's own Unicode-aware case rules, so accented Latin characters and other scripts are handled correctly rather than being stripped or mangled.

**Is my text sent to a server?**
No. Everything happens in your browser as you type, so the text never leaves your device. That matters because what people paste into a case converter is often a real document — a customer list, a draft email, an internal announcement.

**Is there a limit on how much text I can convert?**
The practical limit is your device's memory, since the work is done locally. Ordinary documents of any realistic length convert instantly.

**Does it preserve my line breaks and paragraphs?**
Yes. Only letter casing changes — spacing, line breaks and punctuation are left exactly as they were, so pasted formatting survives the conversion.

**Can I undo a conversion?**
Your original text stays in the input box, so you can switch between cases freely or copy the original back out. Nothing is destroyed by choosing a case.

**Does it work offline?**
Yes, once the page has loaded. Disconnecting from the network and watching it keep working is the simplest way to confirm nothing is being uploaded.

**Is it free?**
Completely. No sign-up, no download and no limit on how many times you use it.

#### Frequently asked questions (10)

**What is a text sorter?**
It is a tool that reorders lines or words. This one sorts alphabetically, by length, in natural order, or at random, and can also clean blanks and duplicates.

**How do I sort text alphabetically?**
Paste one item per line, choose A → Z or Z → A, and the result updates immediately. Turn on Case insensitive if Apple and apple should sit together.

**What is natural sort?**
Natural sort compares the number parts of a string as numbers. item2 comes before item10, which a plain alphabetical sort gets wrong.

#### Frequently asked questions (28)

**What is Base64 encoding?**
Base64 represents binary data - images, files, or raw bytes - using only 64 printable ASCII characters (A-Z, a-z, 0-9, +, /). It exists because many text-based systems (email, JSON, XML, URLs) can't safely carry arbitrary binary bytes, so Base64 re-packages that data as plain text any system can transport without corruption.

**Is Base64 a form of encryption?**
No. Base64 is encoding, not encryption - it uses no secret key and provides zero confidentiality. Anyone can decode a Base64 string instantly, including this tool. Use real encryption (AES, TLS) to protect sensitive data, and Base64 only to make the resulting bytes transportable as text.

**Is my data uploaded to a server?**
No. Every encode/decode runs locally in your browser using native JavaScript APIs. Nothing you type or upload is transmitted anywhere - the one exception is the optional Import from URL feature, which fetches directly from the URL you provide.

**Does this tool support Unicode, emoji, and non-Latin scripts?**
Yes. Text is encoded as UTF-8 bytes using the browser's native TextEncoder/TextDecoder before Base64 encoding, so emoji, accented letters, Hindi, Chinese, Arabic, and any other Unicode text round-trip correctly - unlike tools built on the deprecated escape()/unescape() trick.

**What is URL-safe Base64 and when do I need it?**
Standard Base64 uses + and /, which have special meaning inside URLs. URL-safe Base64 replaces + with -, / with _, and typically omits = padding. Use it for URLs, filenames, cookies, and JWT segments; use standard Base64 for Data URIs and MIME email.

**Why does Base64 make my output about 33% larger?**
Base64 packs every 3 bytes of input into 4 output characters (6 bits per character instead of 8). That fixed 4:3 ratio is where the ~33% overhead comes from - it's mathematically unavoidable, the trade-off for safe, universal text transport.

**Can I convert an image to Base64?**

**Can I convert Base64 back into a downloadable image or file?**
Yes. Paste a Base64 string and the tool decodes the bytes, detects the file type from its binary signature, shows an image preview when applicable, and lets you download the result in one click.

**What file types are supported?**
Any file type, since Base64 works on raw bytes. This tool adds smart handling for JSON, XML, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and SVG, plus binary signature detection for PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, ICO, WebP, PDF, ZIP, and GZIP.

**What's the difference between standard Base64 and Base64 URL-safe?**
They encode the same underlying bytes but use a different character set: standard Base64 uses + and / with = padding; URL-safe Base64 substitutes - and _ and usually drops padding so the string is safe to place directly in a URL, filename, or cookie without extra escaping. This tool's URL-safe toggle switches the output character set; decoding accepts either flavor automatically.

**Does Base64 work the same in every programming language?**
The core RFC 4648 algorithm is universal - a string encoded in JavaScript decodes identically in Python, Java, Go, or PHP. What differs is API defaults: some languages default to URL-safe encoding, strip padding, or require explicit UTF-8 handling.

**Why do email attachments use Base64?**
SMTP was designed for 7-bit ASCII text and can corrupt raw binary in transit. MIME email wraps attachments in Base64 so every mail server can relay them safely as text, and the receiving client decodes them back to the original file.

**What's the difference between Base64 and hashing (MD5/SHA-256)?**
Base64 is fully reversible - you can always recover the original bytes. Hashing is one-way and produces a fixed-size fingerprint that cannot be reversed. Base64 is for safe transport; hashing is for integrity checks and password storage.

**Can I use this for Basic Auth headers or JWT segments?**
Yes, for understanding and debugging - Basic Auth sends "username:password" Base64-encoded (not encrypted), and JWTs are three Base64URL segments joined by dots. To build or verify a signed JWT, use ToolAdda's dedicated JWT Encoder and JWT Debugger.

**Does encoding or decoding change my original file?**
No. Base64 is lossless in both directions — decoding a correctly encoded string reconstructs the exact original bytes, with no quality loss for images or corruption for any other file type. This tool never modifies your source file; it only reads it to produce a new encoded or decoded output.

**Is this tool free, and is there a usage limit?**

**Does this work on mobile devices?**
Yes. The interface is fully responsive and touch-friendly, with a tap-to-browse upload flow and a sticky action bar for quick access on small screens.

**Why do some Base64 strings end with = or == and others don't?**
Base64 processes input in 3-byte groups. When the final group has only 1 or 2 bytes instead of 3, padding characters (= for one missing byte, == for two) are added so the output length stays a multiple of 4. URL-safe Base64 commonly omits this padding entirely since the decoder can infer it from the string length.

#### Frequently asked questions (9)

**How is WPM calculated?**
Net WPM counts every five correctly typed characters as one word, then divides by minutes: WPM = (correct characters ÷ 5) ÷ minutes.

**What is the difference between net WPM and raw WPM?**
Net WPM only counts correct characters. Raw WPM counts every keystroke, including mistakes. If the two numbers are far apart, slow down and aim for accuracy first.

**What is CPM?**
Characters per minute. It is the correct-character count without dividing by five, so 60 correct characters in one minute is 60 CPM and 12 WPM.

**What is a good typing speed?**
Around 40 WPM is average. 50–60 WPM is solid for office work, 70+ is fast, and professional typists often sit at 80–100+. Keep accuracy above 95 percent.

**How can I improve my typing speed?**
Use all ten fingers, keep your eyes on the screen, and fix accuracy before chasing speed. A few focused minutes a day beats an occasional long session.

**Can I type my own text?**
Yes. Turn on Custom passage, paste the text you want to practise, and start typing. The timer and scoring work the same way.

**Is this test free and private?**
Yes. No account. Nothing is uploaded. Only your best WPM and a short local history stay in this browser.

**Does it work on a phone?**
Yes, with the on-screen keyboard. A physical keyboard is still the honest measure.

**What do Tab and Escape do?**
While the test box is focused, Tab restarts the same passage and Escape loads a new one. Those shortcuts do not fire when you are tabbing through the rest of the page.
