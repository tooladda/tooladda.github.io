**What is a QR code?**
A QR code is a two-dimensional barcode that stores data as a grid of black and white squares called modules. A camera reads the whole grid at once, which is why it holds far more than a traditional barcode and can be scanned from any angle.

**How do I create a QR code?**
Pick a type — URL, text, Wi-Fi, email, phone, SMS, contact, location or WhatsApp — fill in the fields, and the preview updates as you type. Adjust colours or error correction if you want, then download as PNG or SVG.

**Is this QR code generator free?**
Yes. It is free, needs no account or email address, adds no watermark, and there is no limit on how many codes you create.

**Do I need an account?**
No. There is no sign-up at any point. The generator runs entirely in your browser, so there is nothing for an account to store.

**How do I create a QR code for a website?**
Choose the URL type and paste your address. If you leave off the scheme, https:// is added; a URL that already names a scheme is encoded exactly as typed, including any query string.

**How do I create a Wi-Fi QR code?**
Choose Wi-Fi, enter the network name and password, and pick the security type. Scanning the result offers to join the network. Characters such as semicolons and colons in the name or password are escaped correctly, which is where naive generators produce the wrong credentials.

**Can I create a QR code for a Wi-Fi password?**
Yes, and it works on both iOS and Android. Be aware that anyone who scans or photographs the code gets your password — it encodes the credentials in plain text, so treat a printed Wi-Fi QR the way you would treat the password written on a card.

**Can I create a contact or vCard QR code?**
Yes. Fill in name, organisation, job title, phone, email, website and address, and the code encodes a standard vCard 3.0. Scanning it offers to save a new contact. Commas and semicolons in your details are escaped so the fields do not split in the wrong places.

**Can I create a QR code for email?**
Yes. Enter an address and optionally a subject and message body. The code encodes a mailto: link, so scanning it opens a pre-filled email. The subject and body are URL-encoded, so ampersands and spaces survive.

**Can I create a QR code for a phone number?**
Yes. The Phone type encodes a tel: link, and scanning it starts a call to that number. International formats with a leading plus and spaces or brackets are accepted.

**Can I create an SMS QR code?**
Yes. Enter a number and an optional message, and scanning opens the messaging app with both pre-filled. The recipient still has to press send.

**Can I create a location QR code?**
Yes. Enter latitude and longitude and the code encodes a geo: URI that opens in the phone's map app. Latitude is validated to between -90 and 90, longitude to between -180 and 180.

**Can I create a WhatsApp QR code?**

**Can I add a logo to my QR code?**
Yes. Upload a PNG, JPG or WebP and it is placed in the centre. Error correction is raised to H automatically, because a logo covers modules that the error correction then has to reconstruct. Keep the logo small — above about 30% of the width the code stops scanning reliably.

**Can I change the QR code colours?**
Yes, foreground and background are both adjustable, and there are presets. The contrast ratio is calculated as you change them and you are warned when the combination is likely to fail — under 3:1 it is refused as unscannable.

**Can I change the QR code size?**
Yes. The export size is separate from the on-screen preview, so you can work with a comfortable preview and still download at 1024 or 2048 pixels for print.

**What is QR error correction?**
QR codes store redundant data so they still scan when part of the symbol is damaged, dirty or covered. There are four levels — L, M, Q and H — with H holding the most redundancy and therefore the least payload for a given symbol size.

**Which error correction level should I use?**
M is a good default for screens and clean printing. Use Q or H for codes that will be printed on packaging, displayed outdoors, or that carry a logo. Use L only when the payload is long and the code will be scanned from a screen in good conditions.

**Why is my QR code not scanning?**
The usual causes are too little contrast between foreground and background, a code printed too small for its data density, a missing quiet zone — the light margin around the symbol — or a logo covering too much of the pattern. Try black on white at a larger size to isolate which one it is.

**What is the quiet zone and why does it matter?**
The quiet zone is the blank margin of at least four modules that must surround a QR code. Without it a scanner cannot tell where the symbol ends, and codes placed flush against coloured backgrounds often fail. Every code this tool produces includes the margin.

**How can I make a QR code easier to scan?**
Use dark on light, keep the quiet zone, print it larger than you think you need, shorten the data so the grid is less dense, and raise the error correction level. Then test it with more than one phone before printing a batch.

**Can I download my QR code as PNG?**
Yes, and PNG is the recommended format for screens, documents and most printing. It is lossless, so the module edges stay sharp.

**Can I download my QR code as SVG?**
Yes. The SVG is a genuine vector — one path built from the module grid, not a PNG wrapped in markup — so it scales to any size without losing sharpness. Note that a centre logo is not included in the SVG export.

**Is SVG better for printing?**
Usually yes. A vector prints crisply at any physical size, which matters for posters, signage and packaging where a raster would show soft edges. For a business card at a fixed size, a high-resolution PNG is equally fine.

**Can I print my QR code?**
Yes. The Print button produces a clean page containing only the QR code and a short label — no navigation, controls or page furniture.

**Do QR codes expire?**
No. A QR code is just an encoding of data; it has no expiry and needs no service to keep working. What can stop working is what it points at — if the URL inside it goes away, the code still scans but leads nowhere.

**Are QR codes permanent?**
The codes produced here are static: the data is baked into the pattern and cannot be changed afterwards. Editing the destination later requires a dynamic QR service that redirects through its own URL, which is a different product with its own tracking and dependency.

**Does a QR code require internet?**
Scanning does not — the data is in the image itself. Acting on it might: a URL code needs a connection to load the page, while a Wi-Fi, contact or SMS code works entirely offline.

**Are QR codes secure?**
A QR code is neither secure nor insecure by itself — it is just encoded data that anyone can decode. The risk is what it points to, since a scanner cannot tell a genuine code from a sticker placed over it. Check the URL your phone shows before opening it.

**Is my QR data stored or uploaded?**
No. The code is generated in your browser by a local library. Nothing you type — including Wi-Fi passwords and contact details — is sent anywhere, logged, or saved to local storage.

**Does the generator support Hindi, Chinese or emoji?**
Yes. Text is encoded as UTF-8, so Devanagari, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, accented Latin and emoji all survive scanning intact. This is worth checking in any generator — a common library default encodes Latin-1 and silently turns non-ASCII text into mojibake.

**How much data can a QR code hold?**
Up to roughly 2,953 bytes at the lowest error correction level and about 1,273 at the highest. In practice much less is advisable: more data means a denser grid, which needs a larger print size to stay scannable. This tool refuses payloads that will not fit and warns when one is getting dense.

**How large should a printed QR code be?**
A common rule of thumb is that the code should be about a tenth of the scanning distance — roughly 3 cm for arm's length, and much larger for a poster read from across a room. Denser codes need to be bigger, so shortening the data is often the cheapest way to shrink the print size.

**Can I use these QR codes for business?**
Yes. There is no licence, watermark or attribution requirement on the codes you generate, and the QR standard itself is free to use. Menus, packaging, business cards and signage are all fine.

**Can I use a transparent background?**
Yes, for PNG and SVG. It is worth testing though — with a transparent background the code takes on whatever is behind it, and a busy or mid-tone surface can destroy the contrast a scanner needs.

**What is the full form of QR code?**
QR stands for Quick Response. The name reflects the design goal: a camera reads the whole two-dimensional grid in a single capture instead of sweeping across a set of lines, so decoding is near-instant. In Hindi it is commonly written as क्विक रिस्पॉन्स कोड.

**Who invented the QR code?**
A team led by Masahiro Hara at Denso Wave, part of the Toyota group, developed it in 1994 to track automotive parts faster than barcodes allowed. Denso Wave chose not to charge for use of the standard, which is why QR codes became universal. QR Code remains its registered trademark, while the specification is published as ISO/IEC 18004.

**What is the difference between a barcode and a QR code?**
A barcode is one-dimensional and stores roughly 20 to 48 characters in the widths of vertical bars, read along a single line. A QR code is two-dimensional, holds up to about 4,296 alphanumeric characters, scans at any angle, and carries error correction so a damaged symbol still resolves. Code 128 is a barcode symbology, not a type of QR code.

#### Frequently asked questions (14)

**Do these QR codes expire?**
No. The code is generated in your browser and encodes your text or link directly, with no redirect service in between. Nothing can stop working later, which is a real risk with free QR services that route through their own shortener.

**Is my data sent to a server?**
No. The code is drawn on your device, so whatever you encode never leaves the browser. No tracking is inserted either — what you type is exactly what is encoded.

**What does error correction actually do?**
It adds redundant data so the code still scans when part of it is dirty, creased or covered. Low recovers from about 7% damage, Medium 15%, Quartile 25% and High about 30%.

**Which error correction level should I choose?**
Medium is a sensible default for printing. Use Quartile for small print or packaging that gets handled. Use High whenever you place a logo in the middle, because covering the centre is deliberate damage that only the redundancy can absorb.

**Can I put my logo in the middle?**
Yes, and it usually looks better. Raise the error correction to High when you do — a logo at Medium may scan on your screen and then fail on a printed poster, which is an expensive way to find out.

**Why does my QR code not scan?**
Usually one of three things: the colours are inverted, so a light code sits on a dark background; the contrast is too low; or the quiet zone around the code has been cropped away. Scanners expect dark modules on a light background with clear margin.

**Can I invert the colours?**
You can, but many scanners will fail on it. If your design needs to be dark, place a light panel behind the code rather than inverting the code itself.

**How big should I print a QR code?**
Roughly one tenth of the distance it will be scanned from. Read at arm's length, about 30 cm, the code wants to be around 3 cm across. On a poster read from three metres, around 30 cm.

**Why is my QR code so dense?**
Because the data is long. Every extra character means more, smaller modules. A URL with tracking parameters produces a much busier code than a short link, so shortening the link before encoding is often the simplest fix.

**What can I encode besides a website?**
Anything that is text. Common uses are Wi-Fi credentials so guests can join without being told the password, contact links for business cards, payment links on invoices, and plain text on product packaging.

**What size and format can I download?**
You can set the pixel size before downloading, and the code exports as an image ready to place into a document, a design file or a print layout.

**Should I test the code before printing?**
Always, and with two different phones. Scanners vary, and the difference between working and not working is often contrast or margin rather than the data. A minute of testing saves a reprint.

**Is there a limit on how much text I can encode?**
The QR standard itself has a limit of a few thousand characters, but long before that the code becomes too dense to scan reliably at normal print sizes. Keep it short — a link is almost always better than the content itself.

**Is this QR generator free?**
Yes. No sign-up, no watermark, no scan limit, and no expiry on any code you make.

#### Frequently asked questions (25)

**What is a barcode generator?**
A barcode generator converts a piece of data into the pattern of bars and spaces defined by a barcode symbology, then renders that pattern as an image. It does not assign or register the number itself — it encodes whatever value you give it into a machine-readable form.

**How do I create a barcode?**
Select the symbology your scanner or standard requires, type the value you want to encode, adjust the size if you have a specific label to fit, then download the result as SVG for print or PNG for screens and documents. The whole process takes under twenty seconds.

**Is this barcode generator free?**
Yes. No sign-up, no watermark, no usage cap and no paid tier. The barcodes you generate are yours to use commercially.

**Which barcode type should I use?**
CODE 128 if you are labelling things for your own systems - it takes letters and digits and is the most compact general-purpose option. EAN-13 or UPC-A only if you are selling through retail and hold a real GTIN. ITF-14 for shipping cartons, and CODE 39 when older or industrial hardware requires it.

**What is CODE 128?**
A high-density linear symbology that encodes any of the 128 ASCII characters. It switches automatically between three character subsets so runs of digits pack two per symbol character, making it the shortest general-purpose barcode for mixed data. It always carries a hidden mod 103 check character.

**What is EAN-13?**
The 13-digit retail product barcode used worldwide. Twelve digits carry the GS1 company prefix and item reference, and the thirteenth is a mod 10 check digit. The first digit is not drawn as bars at all - it is encoded in the parity pattern of the left-hand six digits.

**What is UPC-A?**
The 12-digit retail barcode used in the United States and Canada. Structurally it is an EAN-13 with a leading zero, which is why modern scanners read both interchangeably.

**Can I create a barcode for inventory?**
Yes, and CODE 128 is the right choice. Internal inventory codes need no registration, so you can use any scheme you like - a location prefix, a SKU, a serial number - as long as each value is unique within your system.

**Can I create printable barcodes?**
Yes. Download SVG for print: it is vector, so it stays sharp at any physical size and your label software can scale it without softening the bar edges. For raster, use the high-resolution print preset, which renders at four times the on-screen size for roughly 300 DPI.

**Can I download barcodes as PNG?**
Yes. PNG is lossless and keeps bar edges hard, which makes it the right raster format for barcodes. Choose a print resolution preset first so the file is rendered at the size you need rather than scaled up afterwards.

**Can I download barcodes as SVG?**
Yes, and it is recommended for print and label design. The SVG contains one rectangle per bar plus a text element, so it opens cleanly in Illustrator, Inkscape, Figma and label software, and can be recoloured or resized with no quality loss.

**Can I generate barcodes in bulk?**
Yes. The Batch tab accepts one value per line or a CSV file, validates every row, shows which rows failed and why, and downloads all successful barcodes as a ZIP containing both PNG and SVG for each value. The ZIP is built in your browser.

**What is a barcode checksum?**
An extra character calculated from the others so a scanner can detect a misread. EAN, UPC and ITF-14 use a mod 10 weighted sum; CODE 39 offers an optional mod 43 character; CODE 93 carries two mod 47 characters; CODE 128 always carries a mod 103 check character that is never printed.

**Why is my barcode showing as invalid?**
Almost always one of three things: the value contains characters the symbology cannot encode, the length is wrong for a fixed-length format, or the check digit does not match. The error message names which, and gives the corrected value where one exists.

**Why will my barcode not scan?**
Usually a missing or truncated quiet zone, bars printed too narrow for the printer's resolution, insufficient contrast, or a raster image scaled up after export. Print at actual size from a vector file and keep the quiet zone clear.

**How much white space should be around a barcode?**
At least ten times the narrow bar width on each side for most symbologies, and eleven modules on the left of an EAN-13. It must be genuinely empty - no text, borders or graphics - because the scanner uses it to locate where the symbol starts and ends.

**Can barcodes contain letters?**
Some can. CODE 128 encodes the full ASCII set including lowercase and punctuation; CODE 39 and CODE 93 encode uppercase letters, digits and a handful of symbols. EAN, UPC, ITF and MSI are numeric only.

**Can I customize barcode colours?**
Yes, but carefully. Scanners read contrast, not colour, and most red laser scanners are effectively blind to red bars. Keep bars dark and the background light, aim for 7:1 contrast or better, and never invert. The tool warns you when a colour choice puts scanning at risk.

**Do I need to sign up to create a barcode?**
No. There is no account, no email capture and no limit on how many barcodes you generate or download.

**Is my barcode data uploaded to a server?**
No. The encoder, the renderer and the file export all run in your browser as JavaScript, with no network request involved. Serial numbers, internal SKUs and customer references never leave your device.

**Does this tool give me a real product barcode number?**
No, and no free tool can. A retail EAN-13 or UPC-A number must be allocated from a GS1 company prefix licensed to your business. This tool renders a number you already hold into a correctly encoded image - it cannot issue or register the number itself.

**What is the difference between a barcode and a QR code?**
A traditional barcode is one-dimensional, encoding a short identifier in the widths of vertical bars that a database then resolves. A QR code is a two-dimensional matrix holding far more data - URLs, contact details, Wi-Fi credentials - readable by a phone camera at any rotation.

**Can I validate a barcode number I already have?**
Yes. The Validate tab checks a value against the character set, length rules and check digit of any supported symbology and tells you precisely what is wrong, including the corrected check digit when that is the problem.

**What size should a barcode be printed at?**
Keep the narrow bar at 0.25 mm or wider - about 3 pixels at 300 DPI - and make the bars tall enough that a scanner sweeping at a slight angle still crosses every bar, roughly 15% of the symbol width. A full-size EAN-13 is about 37 mm by 26 mm.
