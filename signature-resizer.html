What Happens When You Press Process

Four things happen, in this order, all on your own device. Knowing them makes it easier to fix a result the portal still rejects.

  1. Auto-crop. The tool finds where the ink is and trims away the empty paper around it, keeping a small margin — about 4% of the signature's width and height — so the strokes never touch the edge.
  2. Clean the background. With "Clean background" on, anything lighter than a threshold becomes pure white and the ink is darkened slightly. The sensitivity slider moves that threshold: higher whitens more shadow and paper texture, but can also thin very light pen strokes.
  3. Fit the exact size. The signature is scaled to fit inside the preset's width and height without being stretched, then centred on white so the file is exactly the size the form asks for — 140 × 60 px for SSC, for example.
  4. Land inside the KB window. The JPEG is saved at up to ten quality levels, halving the gap each time, to find the best quality that stays under the maximum. If even full quality is below the minimum — common with a small, clean signature — a fine grain is added, starting very faint and increasing only as much as needed, until the file reaches the minimum. If the window cannot be met, the file size turns red and a message explains why, instead of quietly handing you a file that will bounce.

If a portal still rejects the file, the cause is almost always a preset that no longer matches the current notification. Switch to Custom and type the exact width, height and KB range from the official instructions.

What Is a Signature Resizer, and Why Do You Need One?

A signature resizer takes a photo, scan, or drawing of your signature and turns it into a file that matches exactly what a form, portal, or application asks for — specific pixel dimensions, and a file size that falls inside a required range. It sounds simple, but most people hit this wall the same way: their phone camera produces a multi-megabyte photo with a huge canvas of white paper around a tiny signature, while the portal wants a tightly cropped image that's often under 20 KB. Getting from one to the other by hand — cropping, resizing, and compressing to a precise byte count — is fiddly and easy to get wrong, which is exactly the gap this tool closes in one click.

This tool runs the entire process locally in your browser: it automatically finds and crops the signature out of the surrounding whitespace, can whiten the paper background and boost ink contrast, resizes to your chosen dimensions, and compresses until the file lands inside both a minimum and maximum KB target — not just under a ceiling, which is where most simpler tools fall short.

Why Government Forms Are So Strict About Signature Files

Government and exam portals process millions of applications through automated systems that expect uniform inputs. A signature that's the wrong pixel size can break a printed form's layout; a file that's too large slows down bulk processing and storage; and — less intuitively — a file that's too small can also be rejected, since some systems treat a suspiciously tiny file as a sign of a corrupted or blank upload. That's why serious portals specify both a minimum and maximum file size, not just an upper limit, which is exactly what this tool is built to hit.

Passport, Aadhaar & PAN Signature Guidelines

Unlike SSC or UPSC, passport and Aadhaar portals don't publish one universal signature-file specification — requirements can depend on the specific form or update you're submitting. PAN card applications more commonly reference a signature area around 2×4.5 cm, which is why a PAN preset is included here. For passport and Aadhaar, use the Custom option with the exact dimensions and KB range stated in your specific application's instructions, rather than assuming a fixed number.

Exam & Recruitment Portals: SSC, UPSC, IBPS, Railway RRB

These are the portals with the most consistently published, exact specifications, which is why they have dedicated one-click presets here. SSC and IBPS commonly ask for a 140×60 px signature between 10–20 KB. UPSC typically allows up to 350×350 px with a wider 20–300 KB range. Railway RRB is usually specified in physical units — 3.5×1.5 cm at 300 DPI — which this tool converts to pixels automatically. Always cross-check against your specific notification, since authorities do revise specs between recruitment cycles.

Banking, University & Scholarship Signature Uploads

Bank account opening, KYC updates, university admission portals, and scholarship schemes each set their own signature requirements, and unlike the exam boards above, there's no single industry standard. The presets included for these categories reflect commonly seen specifications as a sensible starting point — but because requirements vary so much by institution, always confirm the exact dimensions and KB range in your specific form's instructions before submitting, and switch to Custom if they differ.

Understanding the Minimum-and-Maximum KB Window

Most people only think about compressing a file down — but a simple signature on a clean white background can compress to just 2–3 KB even at full quality, which is often below a portal's stated minimum. This tool solves both directions at once: it searches for the highest JPEG quality that still fits under your maximum, and if the result is still below the minimum, it adds a very light, controlled texture to raise the file size without visibly changing your signature or its pixel dimensions.

Signature Dimensions & Resolution, Explained

Pixel dimensions (like 140×60 px) define the exact grid of the output image — this is what most exam portals specify directly. Physical units (mm/cm) are common on printed-form-style requirements and need a DPI (dots per inch) value to convert to pixels; 300 DPI is the standard assumption for signature and photo scans. This tool accepts either: pick pixels for exam-style specs, or millimetres/centimetres with a DPI for print-style specs, and it computes the correct pixel grid either way.

Why JPEG, and PNG vs JPG for Signatures

The final download from this tool is always a JPEG, because that's what the overwhelming majority of government and exam portals require for signature uploads — JPEG compresses far more efficiently than PNG for a mostly-white image with fine dark strokes, which is exactly what's needed to hit a tight KB ceiling like 20 KB. You can upload a PNG (including one with transparency) as your source, but the processed output always has a solid white background, matching what portals expect.

DPI Explained

DPI (dots per inch) only matters when you enter a size in millimetres or centimetres — it's the bridge between a physical measurement and a pixel count. At 300 DPI, one centimetre becomes roughly 118 pixels. Scanning and printing guidelines commonly assume 300 DPI as a baseline for document-quality output, which is why it's the default here; if your specific instructions mention a different DPI, just change the value and the pixel dimensions recalculate automatically.

From Paper to Pixels: Photo vs Scan Best Practices

A flatbed scan on plain white paper gives the cleanest starting point — even lighting, no shadows, high contrast between ink and paper. A phone photo works well too if taken directly above the page in good, even light, avoiding glare and shadows from your hand or the camera itself. Whichever method you use, the "Clean background" option in this tool whitens the paper and boosts ink contrast, which meaningfully improves both the auto-crop accuracy and the final visual clarity.

Common Mistakes That Get Signatures Rejected

  • Uploading a full-page photo without cropping close to the signature first — auto-crop helps, but starting closer improves accuracy.
  • Ignoring the minimum file size and only checking that the file isn't too large.
  • Signing on lined or colored paper, which confuses background-cleaning and auto-crop.
  • Using a blurry or low-light photo — sharpness matters more than most people expect for tiny 140×60 px signatures.
  • Assuming every portal uses the same specs as SSC — always check your specific form's instructions.

Troubleshooting

My result shows "infeasible" or a size warning. This means the target dimensions are too small to fit inside the maximum KB even at the lowest quality, or too large to reach the minimum — try a slightly larger maximum, or a clearer/simpler signature. My signature looks blurry after resizing. Very small target dimensions (like 140×60 px) show flaws more than a full-size photo does — try a sharper original photo or a proper scan. The background isn't fully white. Increase the Clean background sensitivity slider, or retake the photo in more even lighting with less shadow.
