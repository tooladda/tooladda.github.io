    What is an online teleprompter?

    An online teleprompter is a browser-based tool that displays your script and scrolls it smoothly at a controlled pace, so you can read it fluently while maintaining eye contact with a camera lens or an audience, instead of glancing down at notes. Traditional teleprompters are dedicated hardware rigs — a monitor, a beam-splitter glass, and prompting software — used in television studios and professional productions. A browser-based teleprompter delivers the same core experience (readable, precisely-paced scrolling text) using nothing but a screen you already own, which is why they've become the default choice for solo creators, remote presenters, and anyone who needs professional-feeling delivery without a broadcast budget.

    How teleprompters work

    At its core, a teleprompter does one job extremely well: move text past a fixed reading point at a speed matched to how fast you naturally speak. This tool measures the actual rendered height of your script (after applying your chosen font, size and width), divides that height by your target reading duration — calculated from your word count and words-per-minute setting — and scrolls at that exact pixels-per-second rate using the browser's requestAnimationFrame API, which syncs updates to the screen's refresh rate for buttery-smooth motion instead of jerky, timer-based jumps. Because the math is driven by real word count and real rendered height rather than a fixed guess, timing stays accurate whether your script is three sentences or three thousand words.

    Why use a browser-based teleprompter?

    Three practical advantages stand out. First, zero setup friction — open a link and you're prompting in seconds, with no app-store download, no account, and no software update to manage. Second, true cross-platform consistency — the exact same tool works identically on a Windows laptop, a Mac, an iPad propped next to a camera, or an Android phone. Third, and often overlooked, is transparency around your data: because everything runs client-side, you can verify in your browser's network tab that your script never leaves your device, which matters when scripts contain unreleased product details, interview questions, or anything else you'd rather not hand to a third-party server.

    Teleprompter for YouTubers

    Solo YouTube creators are one of the most common users of browser teleprompters — reading naturally from a script dramatically reduces "umm"s, restarts, and awkward pauses that otherwise mean re-recording entire takes. Position your recording device's camera as close to the screen as possible (directly above or below the text works well without a beam-splitter rig), use section markers to jump straight to the segment you're re-shooting instead of scrolling through the whole script again, and set your WPM slightly below your natural conversational pace so you have room to add emphasis and natural pauses without racing to keep up with the scroll.

    Teleprompter for content creators

    Beyond YouTube, creators use teleprompters for podcasts with scripted intros, short-form vertical video (a phone propped in portrait orientation works well with a narrower text width setting), sponsor reads that need exact wording for legal/brand compliance, and course or tutorial narration where precise, repeatable phrasing matters more than off-the-cuff delivery.

    Teleprompter for online meetings and calls

    For video calls and virtual presentations, many professionals run the teleprompter in a browser window positioned just above or below their webcam, at a comfortable font size, to keep key talking points, statistics, or an agenda visible without breaking eye contact by looking down at printed notes or a second monitor far off-camera.

    Teleprompter for teachers and educators

    Educators recording lecture videos or narrated slide decks benefit from consistent, well-paced narration — a teleprompter helps avoid the rambling tangents and long pauses common in unscripted recordings, while section markers make it easy to re-record just the part of a lesson that needs a retake without re-reading the entire script.

    Teleprompter for journalists and news presenters

    Broadcast-style delivery — reading precise, pre-approved wording while maintaining direct camera eye contact — is exactly what teleprompters were originally built for. The reading-guide overlay (dimmed bands with an eye-line marker) in this tool recreates the fixed reading zone that professional confidence monitors use, helping keep your eyes anchored in one place rather than visibly tracking down the screen.

    Teleprompter for public speaking

    For keynotes, conference talks, and formal speeches, a teleprompter reduces reliance on memorization while still allowing a natural, conversational delivery — many speakers use it not to read every word verbatim, but as a safety net of key phrases and transitions (written as short cue lines rather than full sentences) so they always know what's coming next.

    Mirror mode explained

    Physical teleprompter rigs work by reflecting a screen's image off a angled piece of beam-splitter glass positioned in front of the camera lens — the presenter reads the reflection while the camera shoots straight through the glass. Because reflections flip images horizontally, the source screen must display pre-mirrored text so that, once reflected, it reads correctly. This tool's mirror mode supports horizontal (for standard beam-splitter rigs), vertical (for rigs mounted upside-down or at unusual angles), and both combined — cycle through them with the M key or the mirror button in the fullscreen HUD.

    Scroll speed best practices

    Average conversational speaking pace is roughly 130–160 words per minute, though this varies by language, content density, and personal style — technical or statistic-heavy content is usually read more slowly than casual narration. Start around 140 WPM (this tool's default), do a short test read, and adjust up or down in small 5–10 WPM increments using the arrow keys until the scroll feels like it's following your natural voice rather than leading or lagging it. It's generally better to set a slightly slower default and speed up in the moment than to start too fast and feel rushed.

    Presentation delivery tips

    • Do a full read-through at your intended speed before recording, to catch awkward phrasing or a pace that doesn't match the content.
    • Write in short, spoken-language sentences rather than dense written prose — scripts are meant to be heard, not read silently.
    • Use the ((director's note)) syntax for cues you don't want to accidentally read aloud, like pacing reminders or B-roll cues.
    • Break long scripts into clearly labeled [Section] markers so you can jump straight to any part during retakes.

    Video recording tips with a teleprompter

    Position your camera lens as close as possible to the text (directly behind/above/below a monitor, or literally behind beam-splitter glass) so your eye-line reads as "looking at the camera" rather than visibly off to one side. Keep the reading zone narrow enough that your eyes don't have to visibly sweep side to side — this tool's adjustable text width and reading-guide overlay both help constrain how far your eyes travel per line.

    Eye contact techniques

    The biggest visual giveaway of teleprompter use is eyes visibly tracking down the screen as text scrolls. Combat this by using the reading-guide overlay to fix your eyes on one horizontal band near the center of the screen rather than following the whole visible block of text, keeping your camera as close to that band as your setup allows, and practicing brief pauses where you glance slightly up toward the lens rather than staying locked on the text for an entire sentence.

    Script writing tips for teleprompters

    Write the way you actually talk: contractions, shorter sentences, and natural pauses (even just line breaks) all read more fluently off a prompter than formal written prose. Avoid long, comma-heavy sentences that force you to hold a thought across several scrolling lines — break them into two shorter ones instead. Read your draft out loud once before recording; anything that trips your tongue on a first read will trip it again on camera.

    Live streaming workflows

    For livestreams, a teleprompter is most useful for scripted segments — intros, sponsor reads, outros, and any exact wording you can't afford to fumble live — rather than the entire stream. Many streamers keep a lightweight script open in a second monitor or browser window at a small, unobtrusive size just for these moments, switching back to freeform conversation for the bulk of the stream.

    Keyboard shortcuts guide

    While the prompter is running: Space plays or pauses; ↑ / ↓ speed up or slow down; ← / → (or Page Up / Page Down, which most presentation clickers send) jump to the previous or next paragraph/section; Home restarts from the top; + / - adjust font size live; M cycles mirror mode; F toggles true browser fullscreen; and Esc exits back to the editor. The in-app Help panel (the ? button) always shows the current shortcut list.

    Accessibility features

    The editor and controls use semantic HTML labels, visible focus outlines, and touch-friendly minimum tap-target sizes throughout. Font size, weight, letter spacing, line height and color contrast are all independently adjustable to suit different visual needs, high-contrast color presets are available for low-vision readability, and all scrolling animation respects your operating system's reduced-motion preference.

    Mobile and tablet teleprompting

    Tablets are a genuinely popular lightweight teleprompter screen for solo creators — propped on a small stand near the camera, they're far more portable than a monitor and beam-splitter rig. On touch devices, tap the screen to play or pause, and swipe up or down to speed up or slow down without needing a keyboard at all.

    Privacy: what happens to your script

    Nothing leaves your device. Your script is read directly into the page's memory, rendered to the screen, and optionally saved to your browser's local storage so it persists between visits — at no point is it sent over the network to ToolAdda or anyone else. Clearing your browser's site data removes any saved scripts completely.

    Troubleshooting

    • Scrolling feels too fast or slow — adjust WPM before starting, or live with the ↑/↓ arrow keys once running.
    • My [Section] markers aren't showing up — a marker line must be exactly [Label] or start with # through ###### on its own line, with nothing else on that line.
    • Screen turned off/dimmed mid-read — this tool requests a screen wake lock automatically when supported by your browser; if your device still sleeps, check your OS-level display timeout settings.
    • Remote control tab isn't responding — both tabs must be open in the same browser (BroadcastChannel doesn't work across different browsers or devices); reopen the remote tab from the main prompter page.
    • Text looks flipped when it shouldn't — press M (or the mirror button) to cycle mirror mode off if you're not using a beam-splitter rig.

    Best practices checklist

    1. Write in short, spoken sentences and read the draft aloud before recording.
    2. Set WPM slightly below your natural pace, then adjust live as needed.
    3. Use section markers on any script longer than a couple of minutes.
    4. Turn on the reading-guide overlay to keep your eyes anchored for better on-camera eye contact.
    5. Keep the camera lens as close to the text as your setup physically allows.
    6. Do one full countdown-to-finish rehearsal read before the real take.

    Frequently asked questions

    Everything creators and presenters usually ask before their first session.

    What is an online teleprompter?

    An online teleprompter is a browser-based tool that scrolls your script smoothly on screen at a controlled reading speed, so you can read it naturally while looking at a camera or audience — no dedicated hardware or app required.

    Does it work without installing software?

    Yes. It runs entirely in your web browser. There is nothing to download or install on desktop or mobile.

    Is my script uploaded anywhere?

    No. Your script is processed and scrolled entirely in your browser and saved only to your device's local storage. It is never sent to ToolAdda or any server.

    Can I use it offline?

    Yes. Once the page has loaded once, the prompter itself runs entirely client-side with no network calls, so it keeps working without an internet connection.

    Does it support mirror mode?

    Yes. Press M or use the mirror button to cycle through horizontal, vertical, and both — needed for physical beam-splitter teleprompter rigs where the text is reflected off glass.

    Can I control scrolling with the keyboard?

    Yes. Space plays/pauses, the arrow keys adjust speed or jump between sections, Home restarts, +/- change font size, M cycles mirror mode, F toggles fullscreen, and Esc exits.

    Does it work on iPhone?

    Yes, it works in Safari and Chrome on iPhone and iPad, including touch controls — tap to pause, swipe up or down to adjust speed.

    Does it work on Android?

    Yes, it works in Chrome, Samsung Internet and other modern Android browsers, with the same touch controls as iOS.

    Can I upload a script file?

    Yes, you can import a plain-text .txt file directly into the editor, or simply paste your script from any word processor.

    Can I save my script?

    Yes. Your most recent script auto-saves to your browser automatically, and you can also save multiple named scripts to reload anytime, all stored locally on your device.

    Is there a fullscreen mode?

    Yes, the prompter opens in a fullscreen stage by default, with an optional true browser Fullscreen API mode (press F) for an even more distraction-free view.

    Can I change the font size?

    Yes, set it before starting, or press + / - while the prompter is running to adjust it live without losing your scroll position.

    Can I adjust the scrolling speed?

    Yes, speed is set in words-per-minute (WPM) and can be changed before or during a session with the up/down arrow keys, touch swipes, or the remote control tab.

    Does it support wireless presenters and clickers?

    Yes. Most presentation clickers send standard arrow-key or Page Up/Page Down key events, which this teleprompter already listens for to jump between script sections.

    Can I control it from a second device or tab?

    Yes, use the built-in remote control panel — it opens a second browser tab that can play/pause, change speed, and restart the main prompter in real time.

    Does it work for recording YouTube videos?

    Yes, it's well suited to solo YouTube recording — place your camera near the screen, set a comfortable WPM, and use section markers to jump between takes.

    Can I use it for video calls and presentations?

    Yes, many presenters run it in a second window positioned just above or below their webcam during video calls to maintain natural eye contact while reading key points.

    Is there a reading timer?

    Yes, the editor shows an estimated total read time before you start, and the fullscreen HUD shows live remaining time as you read.

    Can I customize the colors?

    Yes, choose from several high-contrast presets (white/black, black/white, yellow/black, amber/black, green/black, blue/black) designed for on-camera readability.

    Does it support long scripts?

    Yes, the scroll engine measures actual rendered content height and computes pixels-per-second from it, so timing stays accurate regardless of script length.

    Does it work on tablets?

    Yes, it's fully responsive and works well on tablets, which are a popular lightweight teleprompter screen size for solo creators.

    Is it free?

    Yes, completely free with no sign-up, no watermark, and no usage limits.

    Can beginners use it?

    Yes, the default settings work well immediately — paste a script and press Start. Advanced settings like mirror mode and the reading guide are optional.

    Are scripts stored on your server?

    No. Scripts are stored only in your browser's local storage on your own device and are never transmitted to or stored on ToolAdda's servers.

    Which browsers are supported?

    Any modern browser — recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all work well, on both desktop and mobile.

    What are the [Section] and ## markers for?

    Any line written as [Section Name] or as a Markdown heading (## Heading) becomes a jump marker, letting you open the Markers panel during a session and instantly scroll to that section.
