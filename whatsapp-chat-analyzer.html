How Each Number in Your Report Is Worked Out

Every figure comes from the dates, times and names in your export file. These are the exact rules, so you know what a number means before you share the card in the group.

💬 Messages

Every line that starts with a date, a time and a name. System lines such as "Aarav added Diya" or the encryption notice have no sender, so they are left out; deleted messages, media and links still count.

🔤 Words

Counted in written messages only: the "<Media omitted>" line of a photo adds none, and web addresses are skipped. A word is any run of letters, numbers and apostrophes.

☁️ Top words and the word cloud

Words of three letters or more, ignoring capitals, after common English fillers — "the", "ok", "haha", "thanks" and the like — are removed. Hindi and Hinglish words such as "hai", "kya" or "nahi" are not on that list, so they often lead a Hinglish chat.

😀 Emojis

Each emoji is counted every time it appears, including several in one message. Emojis built from several symbols, such as families, count as their parts.

⚡ Reply time

When a message comes from a different person than the one before it, the gap counts as that person's reply — but only inside the response window you pick (12, 24 or 48 hours; 24 by default). Longer silences are ignored, so a chat that restarts next week does not wreck the average.

👋 Initiations

A message starts a conversation when it is the first in the chat, or arrives six hours or more after the previous message, whoever sent it. The Initiations column counts how often each person did that.

📅 Span and messages per day

The span runs from the first message to the last, rounded up to whole days. Messages per day divides the total by that span, quiet days included.

🔥 Busiest day and best streak

The busiest day is the single date with the most messages. The best streak is the longest run of back-to-back dates with at least one message, counted by the dates your phone shows.

📎 Media

Found from the lines WhatsApp writes in place of attachments, such as "<Media omitted>" or "image omitted". Android's Without media export uses the same line for every kind of attachment, so its breakdown files them all under images; iPhone exports name each type.

Filters change everything at once. The From and To dates in Advanced options, and clicking a sender to focus on them, recalculate every number and chart from the messages that are left.

What is a WhatsApp Chat Analyzer?

A WhatsApp chat analyzer reads your exported WhatsApp conversation file and turns raw message logs into readable statistics — who sends the most messages, when you chat, favourite emojis, common words, and how fast each person replies. ToolAdda's analyzer runs entirely in your browser, so your private conversations never upload to a cloud server.

Whether you want to settle a friendly debate about who texts more, understand your messaging habits, review a group chat's activity, or export a JSON report for your records, this WhatsApp chat statistics tool gives you instant insights from a standard WhatsApp _chat.txt export.

How to Analyze WhatsApp Chat Online

  1. Export from WhatsApp — open the chat → menu → Export chat → Without media. Save the _chat.txt file.
  2. Upload or paste — drag the file onto ToolAdda or paste export text directly.
  3. Set options — choose date format (auto, DD/MM, or MM/DD) and response window (12–48 hours).
  4. Click Analyze Chat — explore Overview, Senders, Activity, Emojis, Word Cloud, Response Time, Media, and Search tabs.
  5. Download JSON report — save a structured summary for your records.

How to Export WhatsApp Chat for Analysis

Android

Open the chat → tap ⋮ (three dots) → More → Export chat → choose Without media → save or share the .txt file. Android typically uses the format: DD/MM/YYYY, HH:MM - Sender: message.

iPhone (iOS)

Open the chat → tap the contact/group name at the top → scroll down → Export Chat → Without Media. iOS uses brackets: [DD/MM/YYYY, HH:MM:SS] Sender: message. Both formats are parsed automatically.

Who Texts More? Message Counter for WhatsApp

The most common question in any two-person chat: who sends more messages? ToolAdda's chat message counter ranks every participant by message count, shows share percentages in bar charts, and breaks down words, media, links, and conversation initiations per person. For group chats, see which members are most active and which lurk — all from the same export file.

WhatsApp Word Cloud & Top Words

The Word Cloud tab visualizes vocabulary patterns — larger words appeared more often (common stop words like "the", "and", "ok" are removed). Top Words lists exact counts. Useful for spotting inside jokes, recurring topics, project codenames, or how formal vs casual your chat language is over months or years of history.

WhatsApp Response Time Analyzer

Response time measures how long person B takes to reply after person A sends a message (within your chosen 12, 24, or 48-hour window). See average and median response times per person, plus fastest-reply pairs. Note: this is a statistical approximation — overnight gaps and busy days affect averages. Use it for fun insights, not as a relationship score.

Compare message share across members, see who shares the most links or media, identify peak group activity hours, and search keywords across the entire group history. WhatsApp Export Analyzer — Supported Formats

The parser handles iOS bracket headers, Android dash headers, multi-line messages (continuations without timestamps), system messages, deleted-message placeholders, and common locale date formats. If parsing fails, switch the date format dropdown between Auto-detect, DD/MM/YYYY (India, UK, EU), and MM/DD/YYYY (US). Always export Without media — media exports change file structure and increase file size unnecessarily for text analysis.
