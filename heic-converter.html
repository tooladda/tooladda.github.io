The Complete Guide to Converting HEIC to JPG

What Is HEIC?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is the file format Apple has used by default for photos since iOS 11. It's Apple's implementation of the HEIF (High Efficiency Image Format) standard — in everyday use, "HEIC" and "HEIF" refer to the same thing. A HEIC file can store a photo at roughly half the file size of an equivalent JPG, without a noticeable drop in visual quality, which is why Apple made it the default camera format on iPhone and iPad.

What Is HEIF?

HEIF is the broader container format standard that HEIC is based on. It can hold single images, image sequences (like Live Photos), and even bursts of photos in one file, using modern compression that's significantly more efficient than the decades-old JPEG standard.

Why Apple Uses HEIC

Apple switched to HEIC to save storage space — on a phone with limited storage, halving the average photo size lets you fit twice as many photos and videos. The trade-off is compatibility: HEIC isn't natively supported everywhere the way JPG is, which is exactly why converting HEIC to JPG is such a common need.

HEIC vs JPG

JPG (JPEG) is the oldest and most universally supported photo format — every browser, operating system, app, and printer can open it. HEIC is more efficient (smaller files at similar quality) but far less compatible. If you're sharing a photo with someone outside the Apple ecosystem, uploading to a website, or submitting a file to a form that expects an image, converting HEIC to JPG is almost always the safe choice.

HEIC vs PNG

PNG uses lossless compression and supports transparency, making it ideal for graphics, screenshots, and logos — but PNG files are typically much larger than HEIC or JPG for photographs. Convert HEIC to PNG when you need pixel-perfect quality or transparency; convert to JPG when file size matters more than lossless precision.

HEIC vs WebP

WebP is Google's modern image format, and like HEIC, it offers strong compression at good quality. WebP has much broader browser support than HEIC, making it a solid choice for websites that want smaller images without sacrificing the near-universal compatibility JPG offers to non-browser software.

Benefits of HEIC

  • Smaller file sizes — roughly half the size of a comparable JPG.
  • Supports advanced features like Live Photos and image sequences in a single file.
  • Better retention of image detail at a given file size compared to older JPEG compression.

Benefits of JPG

  • Opens on virtually every device, browser, and application without conversion.
  • Universally accepted by websites, email clients, and upload forms.
  • Small enough for web use while remaining widely compatible.

Benefits of PNG

  • Lossless — no compression artifacts, ideal for text, graphics, and screenshots.
  • Supports transparency (alpha channel).
  • Predictable, consistent quality every time.

How to Open HEIC Files

On a Mac or iPhone, HEIC files open natively in Photos and Preview. On Windows, you typically need the HEIF/HEVC extensions from the Microsoft Store, and even then support is inconsistent across apps. Converting HEIC to JPG sidesteps this entirely — a JPG opens everywhere by default.

Convert iPhone Photos (HEIC to JPG)

If you AirDrop, email, or copy iPhone photos to a non-Apple device and they won't open, that's because they're still in HEIC. Converting HEIC to JPG before sharing guarantees the recipient can view them without extra software, regardless of their device.

Best Format After HEIC

For maximum compatibility, choose JPG. For lossless quality or transparency, choose PNG. For the best balance of small size and quality on the modern web, choose WebP or AVIF. For sharing a set of photos as one document, choose PDF.

Image Quality Tips

  • Use 90–100% quality for photos you plan to print or edit further.
  • 70–85% quality is usually visually indistinguishable from the original for web and social sharing, at a much smaller file size.
  • Avoid repeatedly re-compressing the same JPG — each re-save loses a little more detail.

Common Mistakes When Converting HEIC to JPG

  • Emailing HEIC photos to Windows users without converting first — the file often won't open.
  • Uploading HEIC directly to a website or CMS that only accepts JPG/PNG, resulting in a broken image.
  • Using maximum compression on photos you plan to print, causing visible quality loss.
  • Assuming a HEIC-to-JPG conversion tool that requires an upload is private by default — always check.

HEIC File Size vs JPG File Size

A typical iPhone photo saved as HEIC runs roughly 1–2 MB, while the same shot saved as a high-quality JPG can be 1.5–2× larger. That gap is why HEIC exists in the first place — but it also means that once you convert HEIC to JPG, your storage footprint grows. If you're converting a large batch, use the quality slider to balance visual fidelity against the extra space JPG requires, especially before uploading to cloud storage with limited free tiers.

Converting HEIC to JPG for Email, Social Media, and Web Uploads

Most email providers, social platforms, and content management systems either reject HEIC outright or silently fail to preview it. Converting to JPG before attaching a photo to an email, uploading to Instagram/Facebook via a browser, or adding an image to a blog post avoids the "broken image" experience recipients otherwise see. For web pages specifically, WebP or AVIF at 75–85% quality often gives the best balance of small size and sharp detail.

Expert Tips for the Best HEIC to JPG Results

  • Convert in batches by shoot or event so filenames and quality settings stay consistent across a set.
  • Use the filename prefix option to keep converted files organized (e.g., trip2024-) without renaming each one by hand.
  • When resizing, lock the aspect ratio unless you specifically want to crop or stretch the image — unlocked resizing can distort faces and objects.
  • For printing, keep quality at 90–100% and avoid resizing below the original resolution.
  • For a portfolio or client handoff, convert to PDF so every photo arrives as one ordered, easy-to-share document instead of a folder of loose files.
