Rotate PDF pages — the complete guide

What is PDF rotation?

PDF rotation changes the orientation of one or more pages inside a PDF document. Every page in a PDF carries a rotation value — 0°, 90°, 180° or 270° — that tells a PDF reader how to display it. When a scanned page comes out sideways or a photo is upside down, its rotation flag is wrong. Rotating the PDF updates that flag so the page always opens the right way up, in every viewer. Because only the orientation metadata changes, the text, images and vector graphics inside the page stay pixel-for-pixel identical — nothing is re-encoded and no quality is lost.

Correctly oriented PDFs are easier to read, print and share. Phone scans and copier stacks often mix upright pages with a few that came out sideways. Press Landscape in the Select row to pick every page that is currently wider than it is tall, check the highlighted thumbnails, then press Rotate left or Rotate right once. What stays the same when you rotate

The tool edits your original PDF rather than copying its pages into a new, empty file, so bookmarks, internal links, fillable form fields, the document title and the author field all come through unchanged. Only two things change: each page's rotation value, and the page order if you rearranged it. Page content is never re-rendered, so text stays selectable and searchable.

How do I rotate a PDF clockwise or counterclockwise?

Rotate right turns pages 90° clockwise and Rotate left turns them 90° counterclockwise (anticlockwise). Each page also has its own ↻ and ↺ buttons, and 180° turns a page upside down in one step.

Will rotating break a digital signature?

Yes. A digital signature covers the exact bytes of the file, so any change — including a rotation — makes the signature show as invalid or modified. Rotate the PDF first and sign the rotated copy.
