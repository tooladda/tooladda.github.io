Blur images online — the complete guide

What is an image blur tool?

An image blur tool lets you soften or hide parts of a picture so certain details are no longer readable. Instead of installing heavy photo software, you open a web page, load your image and paint or select the areas you want to obscure. Blurring is one of the fastest ways to protect privacy in a photo or screenshot — you keep the overall context while hiding the specific parts that shouldn't be shared. ToolAdda's blur tool runs entirely inside your browser, so the picture is processed on your own device and never uploaded to a server.

How to blur an image online

Start by adding your image: drag it onto the upload box, click to browse, or paste a screenshot straight from your clipboard. Choose a blur mode from the panel, set the strength with the slider, and either apply the effect to the whole image or drag over the exact area you want to hide. When it looks right, pick a format (PNG, JPG or WebP) and download. The whole process usually takes less than a minute and requires no account.

How to blur part of an image

To blur only a portion of a photo, switch to Area blur and drag a rectangle over the region — perfect for a licence plate, a logo or a block of text. For irregular shapes, use Brush blur and paint over the spot with an adjustable brush. Both keep the rest of the image perfectly sharp so the picture still makes sense.

How to blur a face in a photo

Select Brush blur, size the brush to roughly match the face, and paint over it. For several faces, brush each one, or drag an area box around each. This manual approach keeps you in control — you decide exactly what gets hidden, which is often more reliable than automatic detection that can miss faces or blur the wrong thing.

How to blur text in a screenshot

Screenshots frequently contain private text: email addresses, phone numbers, order IDs, account numbers, chat names and locations. Use Area blur or Pixelate to cover each line before you share. Pixelation with a large block size is especially good for text because it removes the fine shapes letters are made of.

Example: Before sharing a receipt, blur the email address, phone number, order ID, card digits, customer name and delivery address — anything a stranger could misuse.

How to blur sensitive information

For IDs, bank statements, medical documents and contracts, treat blur as a first layer, not a guarantee. Apply a strong blur or heavy pixelation over the sensitive area, and for the most critical details consider cropping or covering them out completely so there is nothing left to recover.

How to blur an image background

Use Edge blur to keep the centre of the frame sharp while softening the edges — a quick way to draw attention to your subject and clean up a busy background. For precise control, switch to brush mode and paint the background manually. Note that this tool does not use automatic AI subject detection; you choose the focus area yourself.

Full image blur vs area blur

Full blur softens the entire picture — useful for creative backdrops, censor overlays or privacy screens. Area blur targets a rectangle so only the sensitive part is hidden while the rest stays crisp. Choose full blur for aesthetic effects and area or brush blur for privacy edits.

Blur vs pixelate: which should you use?

Both hide detail, but differently. Blur spreads pixels smoothly, which looks natural and is great for faces and backgrounds. Pixelate replaces an area with large blocks, which is stronger for text and numbers because it destroys letter shapes. For sensitive text, prefer heavy pixelation; for people and scenery, blur usually looks better. When in doubt about very sensitive data, remove it entirely.

Common use cases for image blur

People blur images to share screenshots safely, protect bystanders' faces, hide home addresses in listing photos, cover number plates in car photos, redact documents, prepare tutorial screenshots, and create soft-focus visuals for social media. It is a small edit that prevents a surprising amount of accidental oversharing.

Business and office use cases

Teams blur customer names and order details in support screenshots, hide internal dashboards in demos, and redact figures in reports before sharing externally. Because nothing is uploaded, it's safe to use even with confidential material.

Social media and creator use cases

Creators blur backgrounds for cleaner thumbnails, hide spoilers, obscure logos to avoid copyright issues, and add soft-focus effects to banners and posts.

Student and education use cases

Students blur names and grades in shared results, hide personal data in project screenshots, and prepare clean images for presentations and assignments.

Full-resolution downloads

Large photos are edited on a lighter preview copy so dragging and brushing stay smooth, even on a phone. When you press Download, every edit is replayed on your original file at its full size, so a 12-megapixel phone photo comes back at 12 megapixels with the same blur in the same places. Only very large images are scaled down — above 16 megapixels on phones and 40 megapixels on computers — to stay within the browser's memory.

Privacy and file security

Your image is opened and edited with JavaScript in your own browser using the Canvas API. It is never uploaded, logged or stored anywhere, and nothing remains after you close the tab. The page is served over HTTPS and needs no account or email.

Responsible note: Blur and pixelation reduce visibility but are not a guarantee. Light blur can sometimes be partially reversed, so for highly sensitive information use strong blur or pixelation — and when possible, crop or remove the area entirely rather than only blurring it.

Image blur tips for better results

Use a blur strength high enough that details are unreadable, not just softened. For text, pixelate with a large block size. Zoom in before brushing small areas like faces. Compare with the before/after button to confirm nothing sensitive is still visible. And export as PNG when you need the sharpest result, or JPG/WebP when you need a smaller file.

Will the blurred image keep its original resolution?

Yes. Big photos are edited on a lighter copy so brushing stays smooth, but the download is rebuilt from your original file at full size. Only images above 16 megapixels on phones, or 40 megapixels on computers, are scaled down.

Why does my JPG have a white background where the PNG was transparent?

JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas are filled with white when you save as JPG. Choose PNG or WebP to keep them transparent.

Why is the WebP option switched off?

Your browser cannot create WebP files from a web page, so the option is disabled there. PNG and JPG work in every browser.

Can I undo with the keyboard?

Yes. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac) undoes the last edit, and Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes it.
