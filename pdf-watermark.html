What is a PDF Watermark?

A PDF watermark is a semi-transparent text label or image overlay placed on PDF pages to indicate status, ownership, or confidentiality. Common examples include CONFIDENTIAL, DRAFT, SAMPLE, company logos, copyright notices, and page-number stamps. Watermarks appear visually on the document while the underlying content remains readable — making them essential for legal workflows, brand protection, and document control.

Unlike password protection, a watermark does not encrypt the file. It is a visible deterrent that signals intent: this document is not for public distribution, is not yet approved, or belongs to a specific organization. When you add watermark to PDF files before emailing, printing, or uploading to a portal, recipients immediately understand how to treat the content.

Add Transparent Watermark to PDF

Opacity control lets you create subtle transparent watermarks that do not obstruct reading. Set text opacity to 15–30% for background-style stamps, or 50–70% for clear CONFIDENTIAL labels. Image logos at 20% opacity provide brand presence without dominating the page. Combine low opacity with behind-layer placement for letterhead-style effects on documents with white margins.

Diagonal and Repeated Watermark Patterns

Tiled mode repeats your watermark across the entire page in a grid — essential for leak deterrence because cropping one corner cannot remove all instances. Enable stagger for brick-pattern offsets on alternate rows. Adjust spacing X and Y to control density: tight spacing for maximum coverage, wide spacing for lighter visual weight. The -45° diagonal preset matches industry-standard confidential document styling used by legal and financial firms worldwide.
