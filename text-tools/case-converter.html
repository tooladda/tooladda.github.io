Available Text Case Options & How to Use Them

  • UPPERCASE: Converts every single letter into large caps (e.g., THIS IS UPPERCASE). Great for emphasizing warnings, specific formatting requirements in legal documents, or standing out in plain text.
  • lowercase: Transforms all characters to small letters (e.g., this is lowercase). Ideal for normalizing messy data, cleaning up email lists, or preparing text for database entry.
  • Title Case: Capitalizes the first letter of every word (e.g., This Is Title Case). Perfect for formatting blog post titles, book headlines, YouTube video titles, and academic essays.
  • Sentence case: Capitalizes only the very first letter of the first word in a sentence, leaving the rest lowercase (e.g., This is sentence case.). It is the standard format for most articles, paragraphs, and formal writing.
  • Capitalized Case: Similar to Title Case, it ensures the first letter of every word is capitalized, which is highly useful for names and proper nouns.

Why they are called upper case and lower case

The names are older than keyboards. A printer setting text by hand picked each metal letter out of a shallow wooden tray divided into small compartments, called a case. Capitals and small letters lived in two separate cases, propped one above the other on a sloping stand.

The capitals went in the upper case, further from the hand because they are needed less often. The small letters, used all the time, sat in the lower case within easy reach. The trays are long gone from most print shops, but the words stuck, and "upper case" and "lower case" still mean capitals and small letters.

Why long text in capitals is a bad idea

This is worth knowing before you uppercase a paragraph. Readers do not process words letter by letter — they recognise the overall shape of a word, and ascenders and descenders like the h in "the" or the g in "going" are a large part of that shape.

Capitals remove all of it. Every word becomes the same rectangle, so shape recognition stops helping and reading slows measurably. Accessibility guidance discourages it for the same reason, and some screen readers spell out longer runs of capitals letter by letter, mistaking them for acronyms.

Capitals are fine for a button label or a three-word heading. For a sentence, use sentence case and bold if you need emphasis.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert text to uppercase or lowercase?

Paste your text and choose the case you want. The result appears immediately and can be copied straight back out. There is nothing to install and no button sequence to learn.

What is the difference between Title Case and Capitalized Case?

Strict title case leaves short words such as a, the, of and in lowercase unless they start the title. Capitalized case capitalises every word without exception. Both are widely used — the important thing is being consistent within a document.

What is sentence case?

Only the first letter of the sentence is capitalised, along with anything that would normally be capitalised anyway, such as names. It is the right default for body text, descriptions and most interface copy.

How do I fix text I typed with caps lock on?

Paste it in and choose sentence case. That fixes an accidentally shouted paragraph in one step rather than retyping it, which is the most common reason people reach for this tool.

Why is text in all capitals harder to read?

Because readers recognise the overall shape of a word rather than reading it letter by letter, and the ascenders and descenders that give words their shape disappear in capitals. Every word becomes the same rectangle, so reading measurably slows.

Is uppercase text an accessibility problem?

For anything longer than a short label, yes. Accessibility guidance discourages long runs of capitals, and some screen readers spell out extended capitals letter by letter because they mistake them for acronyms. Use bold for emphasis instead.

Why would I lowercase everything?

Usually to normalise data before comparing or storing it. Lowercasing both sides is the standard way to compare text case-insensitively, and it makes messy imported data consistent before you work with it.
