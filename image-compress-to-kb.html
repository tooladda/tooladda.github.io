Reading the Result — Fits, Over or Too Small

Every photo gets a meter with your limit marked on it. The line under it says which of three things happened, and each one has a clear next step.

✓ Fits 20 KB

The file is at or under your target. You get the highest JPEG quality that still fits, so there is nothing to gain by going lower.

⚠ Under your minimum

It fits the maximum but is smaller than the minimum you set. The photo has too little detail to fill that many bytes — use a larger original, or resize it up in Image Resizer first.

✕ Still over

Only happens with a very small target, when even a heavily shrunk photo cannot get under it. Check the limit on the form — a KB figure is easy to mistype — and try a slightly larger target.

What happens to the photo, in order

  1. Already small enough? A photo already under the target (and over any minimum) is left untouched and downloads with its own name — unless Resize before compress is on.
  2. Quality first. JPEG quality is searched between 10% and 100%, keeping the highest setting that fits.
  3. Then size. If even 10% quality is too big, the width and height shrink by 10% and the search runs again — up to 22 times.
  4. With a minimum set, full quality is tried first, because the largest file that fits is the one least likely to fall under the minimum.
  5. The download is always a JPEG named like photo-compressed-20kb.jpg; transparent areas become white.

Why an exact KB target matters

Most portals reject a file even 1 KB over their stated limit. Instead of guessing a "quality %" like generic compressors, this tool runs a binary search until the output lands at or just under your exact target — so a file you compress image to 100kb for stays at 100 KB or less, never over.

How the compression works

Your image is drawn onto an in-memory canvas. The tool then runs a binary search on JPEG quality (10%–100%) using canvas.toBlob to find the highest quality that stays at or under your target KB. If even the lowest quality is still too large, the image is progressively scaled down by 10% steps and retried until the target is met.

PNG & transparency

PNG images are converted to JPEG with a white background fill, since transparent PNGs generally can't be compressed small enough for strict government form limits.

Compress a Photo to a KB Range — Minimum and Maximum

Many exam and job portals give a range instead of a single limit, such as "photo between 20 KB and 50 KB" or "signature 10–20 KB". A file below the minimum is rejected just like one above the maximum. To hit a range, type the upper number as the Target size and the lower number as the Minimum size. The tool then looks for the largest file that still fits under the target, and each result's meter marks both the limit and the minimum, so you can see the file landed inside the range.

If a photo comes out under the minimum, it simply has too little detail to fill that many bytes — a plain passport photo against a white wall is a common example. Start from the original camera photo rather than a screenshot or a WhatsApp copy, or turn off Resize before compress, and it will usually land inside the range.

Lossy vs Lossless Compression — Which One Do You Need?

Lossless compression (like PNG) shrinks a file without discarding any pixel data — great for logos and screenshots with sharp edges and text, but it rarely gets a photo anywhere near 20–100 KB. Lossy compression (like JPEG) discards visual detail a human eye barely notices, and can shrink a photo by 90%+ while staying visually clean. Because government forms and exam portals almost always specify a strict KB ceiling, this tool always outputs lossy JPEG — it's the only realistic way to compress image to 20kb online for a real photograph without it looking blocky or unusable.

Expert Tips for Best Results

  • Start from the highest-resolution original you have — compressing an already-compressed JPEG twice compounds quality loss.
  • For passport/signature photos, resize to the exact pixel dimensions your form asks for before compressing — it reaches your KB target at noticeably better quality than compression alone.
  • If a form gives a size range (e.g. "20–50 KB"), target the middle of the range, not the minimum — extra headroom protects against re-compression by the portal itself.
  • Keep a copy of the original — once quality is discarded via lossy compression, it can't be recovered.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a screenshot of a photo instead of the original file — screenshots are already re-compressed and re-scaled, so results are worse.
  • Ignoring the form's minimum size — some portals reject a file that's too small as well as too large.
  • Ignoring the width/height requirement — a form asking for "200×230 px, under 50 KB" needs both conditions met, not just the KB target.
  • Re-uploading a PDF screenshot as a "photo" — convert with PDF to Images first if your source is a PDF page.
