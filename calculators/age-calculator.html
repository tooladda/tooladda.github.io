    What is an age calculator?

Instead of manually counting years and adjusting for months with different lengths, the calculator applies calendar rules automatically and returns age in the most useful formats — typically years, months, and days, along with optional totals such as weeks, hours, minutes, and seconds.

    How age is calculated

    Chronological age calculation follows a standard borrowing algorithm used in spreadsheets, government software, and medical systems worldwide. Starting from the birth date and target date, the calculator first computes the difference in years. If the target day-of-month is earlier than the birth day-of-month, it borrows one month and adds the previous month's day count to the day difference. If months become negative after this adjustment, it borrows one year and adds twelve months.

    For example, if you were born on March 15, 1990 and today is July 31, 2026, the raw day subtraction would be negative (31 − 15 = 16 after borrowing from months). The engine adjusts months and years accordingly, yielding 36 years, 4 months, and 16 days. This method matches how age is expressed on most legal documents and is more accurate than simply dividing total days by 365.25.

    When you enable time of birth, the calculator extends precision to hours, minutes, and seconds by measuring the exact millisecond difference between datetime values. Total hours, minutes, and seconds lived are computed from the full elapsed interval, which is why they may differ slightly from multiplying years by fixed constants.

    Chronological age vs biological age

    Chronological age measures calendar time since birth — the number you provide on forms, licenses, and applications. Biological age reflects how old your body appears based on health markers, genetics, lifestyle, and cellular wear — often assessed alongside metrics from a BMI Calculator. A 45-year-old chronologically might have a biological age of 38 with excellent fitness, or 52 with chronic stress and poor sleep.

    This age calculator computes chronological age only. It is the correct metric for legal eligibility, school admission cutoffs, retirement planning benchmarks, insurance quotes, and HR records. Biological age requires clinical tests (epigenetic clocks, blood panels, VO₂ max) and cannot be derived from date of birth alone. Understanding both concepts prevents confusion when medical professionals discuss “heart age” or wellness apps show biological estimates.

    Heart age, mental age, metabolic age, and face age calculators explained

    Searches for a heart age calculator, mental age calculator, metabolic age calculator, or face age calculator usually point to specialized health or AI tools rather than a date-of-birth calculation. A heart age calculator estimates cardiovascular risk age from blood pressure, cholesterol, and smoking status (popularized by NHS and CDC-style risk calculators). A metabolic age calculator, common on smart body-composition scales, compares your basal metabolic rate to population averages for your chronological age. A mental age calculator loosely refers to cognitive-development scoring from psychological tests, and a face age calculator uses AI image analysis to estimate age from a photo.

    Lunar age and Korean age calculator

    Some cultures use a lunar age calculator (also searched as a Korean age calculator) instead of Western chronological age. Traditional Korean age counts a newborn as already 1 year old at birth, then adds a year on each Lunar New Year rather than on the individual's birthday — so a person can be one or two years “older” by this system than by Western reckoning. This calculator reports standard international chronological age (and, separately, Western and Chinese zodiac) rather than lunar-age reckoning, since chronological age is what forms, IDs, and eligibility rules worldwide are based on.

    How leap years affect age

    Leap years add an extra day (February 29) every four years (with century exceptions). Age calculators must respect real calendar dates rather than assuming every year has 365 days. When calculating total days lived, leap days are included naturally because the engine uses actual date arithmetic.

    People born on February 29 — “leaplings” — celebrate birthdays on February 28 or March 1 in non-leap years depending on jurisdiction. Software implementations vary slightly; our calculator uses standard JavaScript Date objects consistent with most web-based tools. For legal documents in leap years, always follow the issuing authority's guidance on which calendar day counts as the birthday.

    Age calculation formula explained

    The canonical years-months-days formula can be expressed as:

    • Years = target year − birth year, minus 1 if the target month/day is before the birth month/day.
    • Months = target month − birth month, adjusted by ±12 when borrowing from years.
    • Days = target day − birth day, plus days in the previous month when borrowing.

    Total days lived = floor((target datetime − birth datetime) ÷ 86,400,000 milliseconds). Total weeks = floor(total days ÷ 7). These totals are useful for milestones like “10,000 days old” or “1 billion seconds lived” — popular social media celebrations that require exact day counts rather than rounded years. For other date math, use our Epoch Converter or Percentage Calculator for proportional splits.

    Age calculator in Excel: the formula for spreadsheets

    If you need an age calculator in Excel or Google Sheets instead of a web page, both support the same approach used here. The most reliable single-cell age calculator formula in Excel is:

    • =DATEDIF(B2, TODAY(), "Y") & " years, " & DATEDIF(B2, TODAY(), "YM") & " months, " & DATEDIF(B2, TODAY(), "MD") & " days" — where B2 holds the date of birth. DATEDIF is a hidden-but-supported Excel function (also available in Google Sheets) that mirrors the same year/month/day borrowing logic this page uses.
    • For age in completed years only: =DATEDIF(B2, TODAY(), "Y").
    • For total days lived: =TODAY()-B2, formatted as a number rather than a date.

    Excel's DATEDIF has known edge-case quirks around month-end dates, so for a quick one-off check — or when you don't want to touch spreadsheet formulas at all — this page's calculator is the faster, error-free option.

    Why accurate age matters

    Incorrect age causes rejected applications, compliance failures, and costly rework. Accurate age calculation matters across many domains:

    • School admission — Cutoff dates (e.g., “child must be 5 by September 1”) depend on exact years-months-days, not approximate age.
    • Government forms — Aadhaar updates, PAN, voter ID, and census forms require DOB matching official records.
    • Passport applications — Minor vs adult categories, validity periods, and parental consent rules hinge on age thresholds.
    • Visa applications — Schengen, US, UK, and other visas ask for age at travel date; errors delay processing.
    • Job applications — Minimum age requirements for certain roles and apprenticeship programs.
    • HR verification — Tenure, retirement eligibility, and benefits enrollment depend on precise DOB.
    • Insurance & loans — Premium bands change at specific birthdays; loan terms can be modeled with our EMI Calculator or Loan Calculator.
    • Medical records — Pediatric dosing, screening schedules, and vaccination timelines use age in weeks or months for infants.
    • Retirement planning — Knowing exact years until a target retirement age informs SIP, PPF, and FD decisions.

    Common age calculation mistakes

    Avoid these errors that cause discrepancies between your calculation and official records:

    1. Using 365 days per year — Ignores leap years; total day counts will drift over decades.
    2. Timezone confusion — Birth at 11 PM in one timezone vs calculation at UTC midnight can shift the calendar day.
    3. Mixing date formats — DD/MM/YYYY vs MM/DD/YYYY causes catastrophic errors; always confirm format.
    4. Rounding months — “36.5 years” is not the same as 36 years, 6 months on legal forms.
    5. Forgetting the reference date — Age “as of today” differs from age on a past exam or travel date.

    Dog age calculator and cat age calculator: pet years explained

    The popular “multiply by seven” rule for dog years is outdated. Veterinary research suggests dogs mature quickly in the first two years — a one-year-old dog resembles a human teenager, not a seven-year-old child. Our Pet Years tab applies a common piecewise formula: each of the first two human years counts as roughly 10.5 dog years, then about 4 dog years per human year thereafter. Cat years follow a similar pattern: 15 for the first human year, 24 by the second, then +4 per year.

    These conversions are approximations for pet owners' curiosity and vet conversation starters — breed, size, and health dramatically affect aging rates. Great Danes age faster than Chihuahuas; indoor cats often live longer than outdoor cats.
